01IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC) · 2026Accepted

Fixed Time Stabilization of Nonlinear Dynamical Systems

Qizhi Lu and Wassim M. Haddad

A control Lyapunov framework for fixed-time stabilization of nonlinear systems. Constructs a universal feedback controller for control-affine systems, characterizes its continuity, and establishes inverse optimality for a nonlinear, nonquadratic performance functional.

Fixed-time stabilization · Control Lyapunov functions · Inverse optimality

Abstract

In this paper, we develop a control Lyapunov function framework for fixed time stabilization of nonlinear dynamical systems. First, we present sufficient Lyapunov conditions for fixed time stability of nonlinear autonomous systems on a prescribed domain and establish a converse Lyapunov theorem guaranteeing the existence of a proper Lyapunov function satisfying a new scalar differential inequality. Next, we introduce the notion of a fixed-time control Lyapunov function for controlled nonlinear systems and construct a feedback fixed-time controller for affine in the control systems. In particular, we provide a characterization for a fixed time control Lyapunov function for affine systems that enables the construction of a universal fixed time stabilizing feedback controller and present necessary and sufficient conditions for continuity of the controller. Finally, we show that the proposed feedback control law is inverse optimal with respect to a meaningful nonlinear-nonquadratic performance functional.

02International Journal of Systems Science · 2026Published

Fixed Time Stability of Continuous Nonautonomous Systems

Qizhi Lu, Wassim M. Haddad, and Kriti Verma

Extends fixed-time and uniform fixed-time stability theory to continuous time-varying systems. Lyapunov and converse Lyapunov theorems clarify how settling-time regularity constrains the regularity of Lyapunov functions.

Fixed-time stability · Nonautonomous systems · Lyapunov theory

Read journal paper ↗
Abstract

In this paper, we develop fixed time and uniform fixed time stability theorems for nonlinear time-varying systems. Specifically, we provide Lyapunov and converse Lyapunov theorems for fixed time stability of continuous nonautonomous systems using Lyapunov functions that satisfy scalar differential inequalities. Furthermore, we show that the regularity of the Lyapunov function is inherently linked to that of the system settling time function. As a result, a converse Lyapunov theorem is presented that can guarantee only the existence of continuous Lyapunov functions. Finally, several illustrative numerical examples are provided to highlight some of the nuances between time-invariant and time-varying fixed time stability theory.

03 AIAA SciTech · 2025 Published

Predictive Cost Adaptive Control of In-Ground-Effect Flight using Active Flow Control with Unmodeled, Unsteady Aerodynamics

Jacob C. Vander Schaaf, Qizhi Lu, Harsh Kumar, Baris Ozmadenci, Krzysztof J. Fidkowski, and Dennis S. Bernstein

Adaptive control for flight close to a moving surface. Online system identification and active flow control address unsteady aerodynamics without a prior control-oriented model.

Adaptive control · Aerodynamics

Read paper ↗
Abstract

In-ground-effect (IGE) flight offers significant advantages in terms of fuel efficiency and stealth. This paper investigates the feasibility of IGE flight using predictive cost adaptive control (PCAC). As an indirect adaptive control extension of model predictive control, PCAC uses recursive least squares with variable-rate forgetting for online, closed-loop system identification. For 2D IGE flight of an airfoil over a sinusoidal wave surface, PCAC uses active flow control with either jet actuation or trailing-edge flap deflection to suppress the effect of unsteady aerodynamics without the need for a prior control-oriented model.

A SmallSat mission study for STARLITE: superluminous tomographic atmospheric reconstruction with laser-beacons for imaging terrestrial exoplanets

Adam B. Johnson, Ashley Padres, Ryan Hughes, Carmine Buonagura, Zane Chapman, Alexia Kubas, Veronica Hegelein, Ishan Mishra, Adler Smith, Abu Taqui Md Tahsin, Akshiti Parashar, Rafid Bendimerad, Deemo Chen, Qizhi Lu, Asterios A. Kounios, Devin Desilva, Verena Padres, James Lloyd, Dmitry Savransky, Joshua Umansky-Castro, Andrew van Paridon, and Elaine Petro

A CubeSat mission concept for imaging terrestrial exoplanets. Five artificial guide stars support atmospheric reconstruction for ground-based telescopes.

Mission design · Space systems

Read paper ↗
Abstract

In the search for life in our galaxy, and for understanding the origins of our solar system, the direct imaging and characterization of Earth-like exoplanets is key. In a step towards achieving these goals, the STARLITE mission uses five CubeSats in a highly elliptical orbit as artificial guide stars to enable tomographic reconstruction of the atmosphere for extreme multi-conjugate adaptive optics (MCAO). Through the use of current and next-generation extremely-large ground-based telescopes, the STARLITE constellation at its ∼350,000 km apogee can provide brighter than -10 magnitude artificial guide stars.

05 American Control Conference (ACC) · 2024 Published

Data-Driven Model Predictive Control of Airfoil Flow Separation

Jacob C. Vander Schaaf, Qizhi Lu, Krzysztof J. Fidkowski, and Dennis S. Bernstein

Adaptive model predictive control applied to airfoil flow separation. The controller learns from closed-loop data and is evaluated with computational fluid dynamics.

Model predictive control · Fluid dynamics

Read paper ↗
Abstract

This paper numerically investigates the performance of active flow control based on adaptive model predictive control without prior analytical modeling. Recursive least squares with variable-rate forgetting is used for online closed-loop system identification. The identified model is then used for receding-horizon optimization based on quadratic programming. This technique is applied to an airfoil model simulated using computational fluid dynamics.

06 AIAA SciTech · 2024 Published

Data-Driven Retrospective Cost Adaptive Flow Control

Jacob C. Vander Schaaf, Qizhi Lu, Krzysztof J. Fidkowski, and Dennis S. Bernstein

Adaptive flow control that learns as it operates. Online system identification allows the controller to start from zero gains without advance data collection.

Adaptive control · Flow control

Read paper ↗
Abstract

This paper focuses on active adaptive flow control. DDRCAC is applied to several 2D internal flow-control problems. Instead of advance data collection, it uses online closed-loop system identification so that the controller adapts from zero gains (cold-start conditions). Hyperparameters are selected based on nominal simulations of fluid dynamics.

Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI) · 2022

End-to-End Vision Based Calories Estimation

Advisor: Oshani Seneviratne

I proposed a depth-weighted calorie-map representation for vision-based food calorie estimation. Using the depth channel of training images, I redistributed image-level calorie labels across segmented food-region pixels, weighting them by depth. This representation enables interpretable, pixel-wise calorie estimates and simultaneous calorie prediction and food segmentation.

University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign · NCSA · Summer 2021

Human Aggression Detection

Mentor: Volodymyr Kindratenko

We used ViViT as a baseline for detecting human aggression in videos and proposed a method for generating synthetic violent-scene training videos. The method segments human contours from existing violent-video datasets and composites them into context videos. This augmentation addresses the scarcity of violent-scene training data by creating additional examples in varied settings.
